## Purpose

How do arable farmers on Schouwen-Duiveland respond as soil and water get saltier and fresh water scarcer towards 2050, and why do farms under the same conditions respond differently? The responses in the model are: carrying on with the current crops and drainage, storing fresh water on the farm, growing salt-tolerant crops, bringing in fresh water from outside, and land leaving arable farming. Which feedback loops make one response more likely than another, and for whom? Storing fresh water and growing salt-tolerant crops are the adaptive practices in the model because each works on a different part of the chain (the water supply, the crop's sensitivity) and each is held back by something in the system; a practice that farmers raise in the group model building session and that fits neither is a candidate to add. The CLD is the picture of the system the whole group works from in the next steps, the semi-quantitative analysis (SIP) and the multi-criteria analysis of LVVN's policy levers. It is not built around one outcome or one set of levers: the group picks those when that step starts.

## Spatial scale

<!-- Draft, for the team to check. -->
The island of Schouwen-Duiveland: its farmland polders, the rainwater lenses under them, the soils, the farms and the land market, and how the water board manages water levels. The island gets no fresh water from elsewhere, so all its fresh water is winter rain stored in the lenses. What is decided at provincial and national level (the water board's funding, LVVN, the Deltaprogramma Zoetwater) comes from outside the boundary: it enters as a policy input, and the CLD shows how it reaches the island.

## Time horizon

<!-- Draft, for the team to check. -->
2050, the year the KNMI'23 climate scenarios and the project look ahead to. The slow parts of the system are judged against that date: the freshwater lens, which a year's recharge barely moves, and land subsidence, which lowers the polders by millimetres a year. The time unit is a year, for every part of the CLD. Each link's description says how long its effect takes to show, from a source, in bands: within the year, years, a decade or more, or beyond 2050; a link slower than a year carries a delay. The seasons sit inside the year, named in the variables they shape (hotter, drier summers; wetter winters; winter rain drained away). A variable that something outside the island sets, or that moves too little by 2050 to matter, is a Constant.

## System boundary

<!-- Draft, for the team to check. -->
The test, from the Purpose (the group's): inside if the farms' responses move it and it moves them back before 2050; outside if it moves the responses and the island cannot move it; left out if it does not move the responses by 2050. Inside the model (endogenous, explained by its loops): the water and salt system (recharge, lens thickness, seepage, upconing, salt in the root zone, soil structure), crop yield and farm finances (revenue, land value, debt, credit, room to invest), whether farmers adopt the adaptive practices, and the objections to re-wetting land. Outside the model (exogenous: they drive it, the model does not explain them): the climate (hotter, drier summers, wetter winters, sea-level rise), EU banking rules (Basel IV), market demand for alternative crops, world prices of conventional crops (what carrying on is weighed against), fresh water supplied from outside and national water policy, and LVVN's levers. Farmland leaving arable use (land no longer farmed as arable land, whoever owns it; a sale to another arable farmer is not part of it) is in the model as one variable, because the land market and succession drive it and water management follows it; what the land becomes afterwards is not. Left out: nature and energy, and other land uses (livestock, vertical farming), which replace arable farming rather than adapt it; and wisselpolders, because the island's waters carry too little silt to raise its polders (see Polder surface elevation). We assume the adaptive practices work rather than model whether they do: the CLD shows what stops farmers adopting them, not whether each one works on the island.

## Assumptions

<!-- Draft, for the team to check. -->
- Each variable is a total for the whole island, and the island's arable farmers are one actor; a group of farms gets its own variables only where a link works differently for it and a source shows that on the island.
- Each variable measures one quantity, named in its description, so the sign of every link into it can be read.
- A link that claims why an actor decides as it does, and has no source yet, is a hypothesis for the group model building session on the island: its panel says so, and the drawing does not mark it. Every other link is to rest on a source.
- A rate measured elsewhere in the delta holds for the island only where its place shares the mechanism's driver: the island borders the Oosterschelde and the Grevelingen, whose tide and silt differ from the Westerschelde's.
- The adaptive practices are assumed to work: the CLD shows what stops farmers adopting them, not whether each one works on the island.
- The labels and descriptions describe what happens in words each actor in it would accept, and no variable is marked as anyone's lever: which outcome and which levers the analysis looks at is the group's choice for that step.

## The named loops

All of them run through one core of {core elements} elements and {core links} links in which every element reaches every other along the links; the other {other elements} elements, {other constants} of them Constants, only drive that core. A core like this has an independent loop set of links minus elements plus one loops, here {core links} − {core elements} + 1 = {independent loops}: a largest set of loops none of which is a combination of the others' links (Kampmann, 2012), of which Oliva (2004) takes the shortest as the simplest description of a model's feedback structure. Neither {feedback loops} nor {independent loops} loops can be read in one picture. Sterman (2000) advises against putting all the loops into one large diagram: build the model up in a series of smaller diagrams, each one part of the story, and number and name the loops the story discusses so the reader can find each. The {named loops} named loops follow that rule: they are the loops that carry the purpose question's story, each running from a change in salt or fresh water, in the farm's finances, or in the uptake of an adaptive practice round to itself. They are not an independent loop set, and the other loops are no less part of the system; they are the ones the story needs. The full drawing marks none of them: a loop's button above the drawing highlights it and shows it on its own.
